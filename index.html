North America holds ~39.6% of market revenue (Grand View Research), but Asia-Pacific is the fastest-growing region in most forecasts — driven by India, Singapore and Japan. Within regions, leaders diverge sharply: UK 33%, Germany 31%, Singapore 34%, Australia 31%.

What's driving it — and what's holding it back

Tailwinds

  • Tool use matured. Function calling, MCP and structured outputs turned chatbots into systems that can transact.
  • Unit costs fell. Cheaper inference makes multi-step agent loops economically viable where they weren't in 2024.
  • Measurable ROI. Early adopters report real cost savings in support, coding and back-office workflows — enough to move budget from experiment to line item.
  • Platform gravity. Hyperscalers and enterprise software vendors are embedding agents natively, removing integration work.

Headwinds

  • The governance gap. Deployment is outpacing controls — audit trails, permissioning and rollback are frequently retrofitted.
  • Reliability compounds badly. A 95%-reliable step run ten times in sequence is a ~60%-reliable workflow.
  • Pilot purgatory. High experimentation, far lower production conversion — the gap between the two is the real story of 2026.
  • Definitional fog. "Agent" spans scripted automation to autonomous planners, making vendor comparison and sizing unreliable.

Market structure

Four layers, different competitive dynamics in each.

Segments

Layers of the stack

  • Models — frontier labs; agentic capability is now a primary benchmark axis.
  • Frameworks & orchestration — planning, memory, tool routing, multi-agent handoff.
  • Infrastructure — sandboxed execution, observability, evals, identity and permissioning for non-human actors.
  • Applications — vertical agents sold on outcomes, increasingly priced per task rather than per seat.
